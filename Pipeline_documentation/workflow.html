Crop-Specific Dekadal Planting-Window Estimation

Per-pixel planting dekad for maize, teff and wheat across the Greater Horn of Africa — fusing Sentinel-2 red-edge phenology, Sentinel-1 SAR and FPAR, constrained by long-term climatology, cross-checked against a FEWS rainfall onset, and closed with a full FAO-56 water balance.

Engine Google Earth Engine + local Python Reference runs Kenya maize (Long + Short rains) · Ethiopia maize (Meher) 2024 Production scale 250 m (10 m = single-AOI ceiling) Countries 11 (GHA / ICPAC) Time step dekad (10 days · 1–36/yr)

01Purpose

Estimate the per-pixel planting dekad (1–36 in a year) for maize, teff and wheat inside crop-specific masks, by fusing Sentinel-2 red-edge phenology + FPAR + Sentinel-1 SAR, constraining the search with long-term-normal (LTN) climatology, cross-checking against a FEWS-style rainfall onset, running a full FAO-56/33 WRSI water balance, and aggregating to administrative units and agro-ecological zones (AEZ).

A dekad is the atomic time step: d1 = days 1–10, d2 = 11–20, d3 = 21–end-of-month; 36 dekads per year. Dekad labels are shown as N · Mon-dN (e.g. 9 · Mar-d3).

04Workflow stages

4.1 Optical & SAR preprocessing s2_preprocess.py · s1_preprocess.py

Per dekad, a cloud-masked median composite of red-edge indices — NDRE1 = (B6 − B5)/(B6 + B5), chlorophyll-sensitive and less soil-noisy than NDVI on sparse early canopy. Sentinel-1 gives RVI = 4·VH/(VV+VH) in terrain-flattened dB with a speckle filter. SAR is mandatory: rainy-season onset coincides with peak cloud, so S1 carries the signal.

4.2 Fusion — gap-free greenness fusion_phenometrics.py · estarfm.py

Production method: cue fusion. Red-edge NDRE + FPAR are primary on clear dates; SAR-derived greenness (RVI) fills the remaining cloud gaps. This is the greenness behind every operational output.

opt_G = mean( unitScale(NDRE), unitScale(FPAR) )
G     = opt_G  filled by SAR-derived greenness (RVI) where optical is missing

ubESTARFM was tested as an enhancement and shelved — a documented negative result for onset. It blends fine/sparse Sentinel-2 with coarse/dense MODIS into a gap-free series, but at matched 250 m resolution it does not beat cue fusion:

Fusion variantScaleHit-ratevs cue @ 250 m
Cue fusion10 m92.7%reference ceiling
Cue fusion250 m81.5%—
ubESTARFM (raw NDVI fill)250 m77.3%−4.3 pts
ubESTARFM (NDVI→NDRE calibrated)250 m78.4%−3.1 pts

Why it loses: MODIS has no red-edge, so ubESTARFM can only gap-fill with NDVI, which rises earlier than NDRE on sparse early canopy and pulls SOS ~1 dekad early. Calibration recovered only +1.2 pts and never breaks even. Resolution is the dominant lever — 10 m → 250 m costs −11.2 pts, dwarfing any fusion choice — so 250 m is the production scale for the multi-country rollout and ubESTARFM stays off the planting path (retained in code for reproducibility).

4.3 Long-Term-Normal priors — rainfall-led, greenness-confirmed ltn.py

The start of season is anchored by rainfall (the dense CHIRPS 25/20 mm onset normal, present every season) and confirmed by greenness (the MODIS greenup normal, averaged in only where present). The gate constrains SOS to ±ltn_pad dekads only where a prior exists — elsewhere it passes through to the calendar window, so a sparse second-cycle phenology can never mask a season out. The temperature LTN makes the emergence offset spatial.

Validation — season behaviour (measured) Where both signals are strong (long rains) the prior adds real skill: +4.5 pts calendar hit-rate (91.4% → 95.9%). Where the signal is intrinsically weak (short rains: sparse Greenup_2, noisy onset) the rainfall-led prior keeps the season running — 78% of pixels retained vs 0.02% under a naïve phenology gate — but does not sharpen it (59.8% → 53.4%). The short rains are confirmation-limited: an honest data limitation, not a code defect. Lean on the rainfall onset there.

4.4 SOS detection & planting date fusion_phenometrics.py · planting_date.py

Definition SOS = the earliest dekad where G crosses baseline + 25%·amplitude and the dekad-to-dekad slope is positive (sustained green-up), within the calendar window intersected with the LTN prior. The crop mask is applied once, at the SOS output.
planting_dekad = SOS_dekad − emergence_offset      # maize 2, wheat 1, teff 1 (dekads)

The offset is the planting → detectable green-up lag (satellite, ~2 dekads for maize), not the ~7-day physical emergence — a 10-day dekad cannot resolve 7 days. With the temperature LTN on, the offset varies spatially.

4.5 FEWS rainfall onset wrsi_feedback.py

The 25/20 mm rule (Senay & Verdin 2003) plus a new P/PET ≥ 0.5 agroclimatic gate that suppresses spurious onsets in high-evaporative-demand zones. The 6-obs + 4-forecast dekad (Open-Meteo, free) completes the running dekad for near-real-time timeliness; retrospective runs use full observed CHIRPS.

4.6 WRSI water balance wrsi_waterbalance.py · soil.py

ET₀  = Hargreaves(ERA5-Land Tmin/Tmax, Ra(lat, DOY))
WR   = Kc(days-since-planting) · ET₀
Wb   = SW + P ;  AET = min(Wb, WR) ;  SW = min(Wb − AET, WHC)
WRSI = 100 · ΣAET / ΣWR        # + deficit (mm) + FEWS class 1–5

Full FAO-56/33 dekadal balance, started at each pixel's detected planting dekad. Spatial WHC from OpenLandMap field capacity − wilting point over the crop root zone.

WRSIClassInterpretation for maize
≥ 955No/very-mild deficit — optimal
80–954Good — minimal yield reduction
60–803Mediocre
50–602Poor
< 501Crop failure

"Good enough" for maize = WRSI ≥ 80 (≥ 95 = no stress; < 50 = failure). WRSI is a whole-cycle verdict; at the start of season water adequacy is the onset rule (25/20 mm + P/PET ≥ 0.5), not WRSI. A QGIS style (wrsi_classes_*.qml) ships these breaks.

AEZ × WRSI cross-validation Water satisfaction rises with maturity class exactly as agronomy predicts — the water balance independently confirms the AEZ → maturity logic: early (dry lowland) WRSI 90.0 / 4.2% failure · medium 93.8 / 0.8% · late (humid highland) 97.1 / 0%.

4.7 Zonal aggregation & AEZ maturity zonal_aggregate.py · aez_analysis.py

Key finding · Kenya maize, Long rains 2024 Planting timing is nearly uniform across AEZ (~dekad 9, Mar-d3); the maturity class is what varies by zone. The planting-dekad map shows timing; the AEZ map shows maturity — largely decoupled.

06Outputs

  • Rasters (GeoTIFF): planting dekad; WRSI + deficit + performance class.
  • Tables (CSV / CSV-with-WKT): admin-1/2/3 planting distribution + skill; AEZ maturity; per-admin attribute table (NDRE / S1 / S2 / FPAR + LTN normals + SOS/planting).
  • Cartography (PDF/PNG): planting-dekad maps, modal-dekad & hit-rate choropleths, skill graphs, AEZ-influence figure.

07Key design decisions

  • SOS ≠ planting — planting precedes detectable green-up by a crop-specific satellite lag.
  • Red-edge (NDRE) over NDVI — chlorophyll-sensitive, less soil noise on sparse early canopy.
  • SAR is mandatory — rainy-season onset = peak cloud; S1 carries the signal.
  • LTN prior gates the search — rejects weeds / second-flush false starts.
  • WRSI runs entirely in GEE — no GeoWRSI hand-off; planting dekad feeds the balance as SOS.

08Caveats

  • Calendar windows in season_calendar.csv are indicative — calibrate against FEWS NET / FAO GIEWS / GEOGLAM.
  • Smallholder mixed pixels blur SOS; resolution is the dominant skill lever — ubESTARFM was tested and does not help onset, so reach for native-10 m S2 or Planet 3 m, not spatiotemporal fusion.
  • AEZ maturity mapping is a first-order Jaetzold convention — calibrate against Kenya Seed variety zonation.
  • Ward-level (admin-3) stats from downsampled mosaics are approximate; use native-resolution zonal stats for publication.
  • Full LTN + WRSI at 10 m is compute-heavy and did not sustain for two concurrent country exports (both 10 m runs failed after 2+ days) — 250 m is the production scale; reserve 10 m for single-AOI reference runs on a paid project.
